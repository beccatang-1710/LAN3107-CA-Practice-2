Student A

Task

You are Charlie Chan, a marketing assistant at HealthFirst Medical. You are at a company event. Hold a conversation with your colleague, Parker Leung (Student B, a centre operations assistant). You should perform all items below:

  1. Greet your colleague and introduce yourself
  2. Make small talk on the following topics:
    • Your favourite local restaurants
    • A project run by your department

    Project: Promoting mobile health clinics

    You may talk about:

    • what has been done so far
    • plans for the coming months
  3. Share your opinions on a new company policy and support your views with reasons

    Your company has just introduced a professional development policy. Employees now have access to free online courses and training workshops.

    You may talk about:

    • what you think or feel about the policy and why
    • the potential benefits and drawbacks the policy may bring to the company and its employees
  4. End the conversation politely

Student B

Task

You are Parker Leung, a centre operations assistant at HealthFirst Medical. You are at a company event. Hold a conversation with your colleague, Charlie Chan (Student A, a marketing assistant). You should perform all items below:

  1. Greet your colleague and introduce yourself
  2. Make small talk on the following topics:
    • Your favourite local restaurants
    • A project run by your department

    Project: Launching a new patient appointment scheduling system

    You may talk about:

    • what has been done so far
    • plans for the coming months
  3. Share your opinions on a new company policy and support your views with reasons

    Your company has just introduced a professional development policy. Employees now have access to free online courses and training workshops.

    You may talk about:

    • what you think or feel about the policy and why
    • the potential benefits and drawbacks the policy may bring to the company and its employees
  4. End the conversation politely

1. Greet your colleague and introduce yourself
'Hi. I don't think we've met yet. I'm Charlie Chan and I'm from the marketing department. It's nice to meet you.'
'Hi Charlie. I'm Parker Leung from the centre operations department. It's nice to meet you too. How are you enjoying the event so far?'
Greeting · Introducing yourself · Making small talk (follow-up question)
Contractions — "I'm" (I am) / "we've" (we have) / "It's" (It is) Follow-up question (Wh-) — "How are you enjoying the event so far?"
2. Make small talk on the following topics: a. Your favourite local restaurants
'I'm having a good time, thanks. It's always nice to catch up with colleagues from other departments. The food here at the event is pretty good, isn't it? Speaking of food, do you have any favourite local restaurants you like to visit?'
'Yes, I do! I really enjoy going to "The Green Bowl" near our office. Their vegetarian dishes are amazing. How about you?'
Responding to small talk · Giving positive comments · Asking follow-up questions
Short answer / echo — "Yes, I do!" Positive comments — "amazing" Tag question — "The food here is pretty good, isn't it?" Follow-up question (yes/no) — "Do you have any favourite…?" Follow-up question — "How about you?" Cohesive device — "Speaking of…"
'Oh, I love "Dim Sum Delight" in Central. Their steamed dumplings are the best I've ever had. Maybe we could go together sometime.'
'That sounds great! I've heard good things about that place but haven't tried it yet. So, what's new in your department these days?'
Sharing preferences · Making a suggestion · Echoing · Bringing up a new topic
Echo words — "That sounds great!" Follow-up question (Wh-) — "What's new in your department…?" Present perfect (experience) — "I've ever had" / "I've heard" Contraction — "haven't" (have not) Cohesive device — "So, …" Modal verb (suggestion) — "could go"
b. A project run by your department — Project: Promoting mobile health clinics
'Oh, we've been busy promoting our mobile health clinics. We've launched a social media campaign and partnered with local community groups to spread the word. The response has been encouraging.'
'That's impressive! What are your plans for the mobile clinics in the next few months?'
Talking about work projects — what has been done so far · Asking about future plans
Present perfect (task completed) — "We've launched…" Present perfect continuous — "we've been busy promoting…" Giving purpose — "to spread the word" Contraction — "That's" (That is) Follow-up question (Wh-) — "What are your plans…?"
'We're planning to expand the clinics to more districts and introduce new health services. We hope to reach more people who need medical care.'
'That's a great initiative. I think it will really benefit the community.'
Talking about work projects — future plans · Giving positive comments · Expressing an opinion
Future tense / present continuous (plans) — "We're planning to expand…" Future intention — "We hope to reach…" Expressing opinions — "I think" Modal verb (prediction) — "will benefit" Contraction — "That's" (That is)
'So how about you? Busy with new projects, too?'
'We're currently working on launching a new patient appointment scheduling system. It's been a big project for us.'
Asking a follow-up question · Talking about work projects — work in progress
Follow-up question — "how about you?" Present continuous (task in progress) — "We're currently working…" Present perfect — "It's been" (It has been)
'Oh, that must be a big task! I can imagine how much planning and coordination goes into setting up a new system like that. How has the process been so far?'
'We've completed the initial design and started testing the system with a small group of patients. The feedback has been positive so far. We plan to roll it out to all our centres next quarter. We're also preparing training materials for staff to make the transition smooth.'
Showing empathy · Asking about progress · Talking about work projects — three tenses
Present perfect (task completed) — "We've completed… / so far" Future tense (work to complete) — "We plan to roll it out…" Present continuous (task in progress) — "We're also preparing…" Modal verbs — "must be" (deduction) / "can imagine" (ability) Giving purpose — "to make the transition smooth"
3. Share your opinions on a new company policy and support your views with reasons
'Sounds like a lot of work, but very rewarding. Speaking of new things, what do you think about the new professional development policy our company introduced?'
'I think it's a fantastic idea. Offering free online courses and training workshops shows the company cares about our growth.'
Showing empathy · Starting a conversation about a company policy · Expressing an opinion (positive)
Starting a conversation — "What do you think about…?" Expressing opinions — "I think" Positive comments — "fantastic" Cohesive device — "Speaking of…" Contraction — "it's" (it is)
'Definitely. It's a good opportunity for us to learn new skills. I'm planning to take a course on digital marketing.'
'That would be perfect for your role. Do you see any drawbacks to the new policy?'
Expressing agreement · Sharing personal plans · Inviting opinions on drawbacks
Expressing agreement — "Definitely" Future / present continuous (plans) — "I'm planning to take…" Modal verb (hypothetical) — "would be" Inviting opinions — "Do you see any drawbacks…?" Contraction — "It's" (It is)
'One concern is that some employees might feel pressured to take courses even if they're already busy. It's important to balance work and learning.'
'True, time management could be a challenge. But overall, I think the benefits outweigh the drawbacks. It can help us stay competitive.'
Stating a drawback · Explaining reasons · Expressing overall opinion
Modal verbs (possibility) — "might feel" / "could be" / "can help" Expressing opinions — "I think" Cohesive device — "But overall, …" Contraction — "they're" (they are)
'Absolutely. It also encourages a culture of continuous improvement, which is good for both employees and the company.'
'I agree! It could lead to more collaboration and innovation within the company and I'm excited to see how things develop over the next few months.'
Expressing agreement · Elaborating on benefits
Agreement — "Absolutely" Positive verb — "It also encourages" Agreement — "I agree" Modal verb — "It could lead to" (possibility) Contraction — "I'm" (I am)
'Well, Parker, it's been great chatting with you. I've enjoyed learning more about your department and your thoughts on the new policy.'
'Likewise, Charlie. It's always nice to exchange ideas and catch up. Let's keep in touch about those restaurant plans!'
Wrapping up · Showing appreciation · Responding politely · Suggesting future contact
Echo words — "Likewise" Present perfect — "it's been" / "I've enjoyed" Contractions — "It's" (It is) / "Let's" (Let us) Ending politely — "Let's keep in touch."
4. End the conversation politely
'Absolutely! Let's arrange a lunch soon. Enjoy the rest of the event.'
'You too, Charlie. Take care and see you at the office!'
Agreeing to keep in touch · Ending the conversation politely
Expressing agreement — "Absolutely" Contraction — "Let's" (Let us) Ending politely — "Take care and see you at the office!"
